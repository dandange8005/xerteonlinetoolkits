The role of family in providing physical and psychosocial support of patients with cancer was identified in a review by Coyne and colleagues (Click here to access).​

Our review found that involving family during physical activities such as walking and exercise routines provided companionship, encouragement and motivation, and psychological support (Click here to access).​

​The importance of involving family was recognised by an Occupational Therapist working in a prehab service: ​

The importance of involving loved ones was also recognised in our workshops. They suggested the following:​

  • ​Having a family member present when being told about cancer prehab.​
  • Having a friend or family member accompany them to prehab appointments.​
  • Including family in conversations, especially if they're involved in monitoring progress.​

​Ways to encourage and support family and friends include:​

  • Advise people with cancer to spend time with their family and friends regularly to discuss needs and progress.​
  • Caregivers need relief and rest to stay emotionally and physically fit and be able to help the person with cancer. ​
  • Advise caregivers to not feel guilty taking a break - brief time away can allow carers to recharge their batteries.​
