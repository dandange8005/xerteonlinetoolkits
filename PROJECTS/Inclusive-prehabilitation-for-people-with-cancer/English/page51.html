Our literature review identified key incentives for accessing and adhering to cancer prehabilitation (Click here to read it). These included the four themes below. These themes were also supported by comments made by participants in both phase two and three of the research.

Checkout the cards below for more information on the themes with supporting statements.

Benefits of Prehab

Patients' beliefs that engagement might provide personal benefit e.g. improved health/independence and reduced burden on healthcare.

Clinician Support

Clinicians providing endorsement and encouraging prehab can increase uptake.

“It sounds like she’s already taken [the surgeon’s] advice into consideration and starting to try and increase her physical activity at home…”

Past Experiences

Linking to past personal activities or behaviours can make prehab more achievable.

“She's already going to yoga, Pilates and Zumba. That she's already aware of them and quite confident to go to exercise [classes]. She's scored 10 out of 10 on the confidence scale. So, she's not nervous or anxious about going to class.”

Our workshops suggested it was important to ‘avoid shaming about lifestyle’ such as smoking.

But it has to be clear to the person that it’s not putting blame on you, it’s telling you, ‘In order for you to go through your treatment, these things may benefit you and enhance your treatment.’ You’re not looking at what’s gone on before, this is about what is going to happen in the future.”
