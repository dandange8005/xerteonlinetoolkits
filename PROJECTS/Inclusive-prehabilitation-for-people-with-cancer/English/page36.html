Successful prehabilitation depends on changing aspects of behaviour, including attendance at, engagement with and adherence to the exercise, nutrition and psychological components of prehabilitation. 

Communication techniques to facilitate behaviour change include:​

  • Motivational interviewing ​
  • Inviting SMART goals and action planning​
  • Self-monitoring, follow-up and feedback​
  • Follow up and feedback
  • Celebrate

Motivational interviewing: a person-centred communication approach that helps people to: ​

  • Focus; ​
  • Suggest their own reasons for change;​
  • Explore and resolve ambivalence to change;​
  • Enhance intrinsic motivation and planning;​
  • Explore importance of behaviour change to the person;​
  • Then to explore their confidence to change behaviour AND what they need to improve their confidence.

Inviting SMART goals and action planning​

SMART goals: ​

  • Specific ​
  • Measurable ​
  • Achievable ​
  • Realistic ​
  • Timebound ​

SMART goals help people focus on realistic and meaningful changes. 

Action plans:

  • Break goals into smaller, realistic, manageable and achievable steps. ​
  • Achievable behaviours enhance opportunity for success and may increase confidence.

In one interview, an occupational therapist said:

"It's planning, prioritising, pacing, permission and positioning."

"So, the pacing is to look at a task and pace it out over a longer period of time rather than doing all in one go. So doing more smaller pieces of work to achieve the same aim. So, thinking about how you break a task down […]
"Planning and prioritising, kind of look at them together. So, you're prioritising things either throughout the week of the tasks that you desperately have to do or want to do and planning how you're going to do each of those. So, is it a priority for today or is it a priority for some time this week when I have the energy? So, it's looking at all the tasks you need to do, planning out what you need to do and then slotting them in as to when you can do them. So, planning and prioritising."
"Positioning is thinking about how you're doing things. Are you doing everything in standing? If you're doing some gardening, if you're looking at your pots, could you sit down and do that rather than test everything in standing, balance and everything else? It's even positioning of where you have things laid out in your home. If it's in the kitchen and you're constantly bending and stretching, as I said to her, it's about having things to hand a bit more. Do you have a chair in the kitchen that you can then sit down while you take a break, or can you do tasks sitting down rather than in standing?"
"And then permission is talking about permission to ask for help. So it's okay to take a break, giving yourself permission for that break, that it isn't a luxury."

Self-monitoring:​

  • Encouraging people to track behaviours such as physical activity, food intake and emotional state can provide them with insight into their habits and progress.​

Follow up and feedback:

  • Regular feedback helps people stay motivated and adjust strategies as needed.

Celebrate:​

  • Acknowledging and celebrating prehabilitation achievements can build individuals’ self-efficacy in prehabilitation activities.
