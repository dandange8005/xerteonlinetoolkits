slide 1 In one interview, an occupational therapist said: It's planning, prioritising, pacing, permission and positioning. slide 2 So, the pacing is to look at a task and pace it out over a longer period of time rather than doing all in one go. So doing more smaller pieces of work to achieve the same aim. So, thinking about how you break a task down (…) . slide 3 "Planning and prioritising, kind of look at them together. So, you're prioritising things either throughout the week of the tasks that you desperately have to do or want to do and planning how you're going to do each of those. So, is it a priority for today or is it a priority for some time this week when I have the energy? So, it's looking at all the tasks you need to do, planning out what you need to do and then slotting them in as to when you can do them. So, planning and prioritising." slide 4 "Positioning is thinking about how you're doing things. Are you doing everything in standing? If you're doing some gardening, if you're looking at your pots, could you sit down and do that rather than test everything in standing, balance and everything else? It's even positioning of where you have things laid out in your home. If it's in the kitchen and you're constantly bending and stretching, as I said to her, it's about having things to hand a bit more. Do you have a chair in the kitchen that you can then sit down while you take a break, or can you do tasks sitting down rather than in standing?" slide 5 "And then permission is talking about permission to ask for help. So it's okay to take a break, giving yourself permission for that break, that it isn't a luxury."
