We systematically reviewed 56 previous research studies about the factors influencing prehab take up (Click here to access research). 

The findings were organised into 3 themes:

  1. The person
  2. The prehab service
  3. Interpersonal support

We’ll look at each in turn…

1. The person

People with cancer are more likely to take up prehab if:

  • They think it will benefit them
  • They are motivated about their future (e.g. being around for family)

Cancer can be life-changing but people with cancer have lots of other demands, such as:

  • Other health conditions
  • Family, work, and financial commitments
  • Transport issues

These must be considered if people are to take up prehab

2. The prehab service

The way services are delivered can help or hinder uptake.

For example, remote (online/telephone) services can facilitate access, but many people may prefer face-to-face services.

Services must:

  • Be user-friendly*
  • Meet the needs, preferences and capabilities of individual people*

[*Don’t worry – we’ll cover how to do this in later modules]

The level of supervision by health professionals should be tailored, but we know that funding and staff shortages can limit this.

3. Interpersonal support

Support of others was the most important factor helping (or hindering) prehabilitation uptake:

  • Family and friends
  • Cancer care workers
  • Fellow people with cancer
  • Volunteers and support groups

Supportive social networks was particularly important for encouraging physical activity e.g. exercising with friends.
