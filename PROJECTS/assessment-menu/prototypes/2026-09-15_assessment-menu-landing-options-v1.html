      Guidance, not policy

      This menu doesn't tell you which assessment to use. Decisions about assessment types and lanes are made by module and programme teams, based on the learning outcomes.

      Two lanes, no middle lane

      The two lanes

      Every assessment is either Lane 1 or Lane 2. Both are robust ways to assess, and most programmes need both. There's no set number of Lane 1 assessments.

      1

      Lane 1

      Secure assurance of learning

      • Gives trustworthy evidence of what students can do in their own right.
      • Includes at least one assurance feature: controlled conditions, a task that can't be answered generically, or a check such as questioning or a viva.
      • Doesn't have to be an exam.
      2

      Lane 2

      Open assessment for and as learning

      • Open, supported assessment that helps students learn, including how to use AI well.
      • Students may use AI as the brief sets out. Their reasoning, judgement and use of sources stay visible.
      • Students remain responsible for the work they submit.

      Before you browse

      Start with the learning outcome

      Instead of asking "What assessment should I use?", ask:

      1. What do students need to demonstrate?
      2. Why is this assessment the best way to show that learning?
      3. How authentic is the task to the discipline, profession or wider world?
      4. How can we reduce barriers to taking part and succeeding?
      5. What role, if any, should generative AI play?
      6. How does this assessment fit into the wider programme?

      Inclusive by design

      Students don't have to use AI

      Proposed key message D1
      The two-lane approach doesn't assume that students must use generative AI. Where AI is allowed, the brief should say whether using it is optional or part of the task. Design assessments so that students aren't disadvantaged if they choose not to use AI, for ethical, environmental, personal, accessibility or academic reasons.
