Written Assessments

Traditional and contemporary written tasks, from essays to in-class tests.

Essay

Extended written argument or analysis on a set question or topic.

Lane 2
▾
🔒 Configuring for Lane 1

In its standard take-home form, an essay is not securable. To move to Lane 1, consider an in-class timed essay under invigilation, or pair with a viva or oral follow-up to verify authorship of submitted work.

🤝 Configuring for Lane 2

Design with AI use acknowledged. Ask students to submit a process log, annotated bibliography, or AI use declaration alongside the essay. Focus marking criteria on analytical depth and original argument — things AI struggles to produce at quality.

In-class / Timed Written Test

Supervised written task completed within a set time under exam conditions.

Lane 1
▾
🔒 Configuring for Lane 1

Naturally suited to Lane 1. Ensure the task is genuinely programme-level and tests the learning outcomes you need to validate — avoid using it simply because it is securable. Consider open-book formats to test application rather than recall.

🤝 Configuring for Lane 2

Less common in Lane 2, but a take-home version of a timed task — where students work independently but not under supervision — can function as Lane 2 if the design focuses on process and reflection alongside output.

Report

Structured written output presenting research, analysis, or professional recommendations.

Lane 2
▾
🔒 Configuring for Lane 1

A take-home report is not securable on its own. Pair with an in-person defence, short viva, or poster presentation where students must explain and justify their findings without notes or AI assistance.

🤝 Configuring for Lane 2

Well suited to Lane 2. Design the brief so that the quality of critical analysis and decision-making is foregrounded. Require a methodology section or AI use log so students account for how they produced the work.

Reflective Log / Journal

Ongoing or end-of-module personal reflection on learning, practice, or experience.

Lane 2
▾
🔒 Configuring for Lane 1

Difficult to use as a standalone Lane 1 assessment. Reflective writing produced outside supervision cannot easily be secured. Could be incorporated into a Lane 1 context via in-class reflective writing or an oral follow-up on written reflections.

🤝 Configuring for Lane 2

Naturally suited to Lane 2. Ongoing logs that record genuine personal development are harder to fabricate at quality. Encourage students to include specific incidents, dates, and critical analysis of their own practice.

Oral & Verbal Assessments

In-person or live spoken assessments — some of the most effective Lane 1 options.

Viva / Oral Examination

Structured academic conversation testing depth of knowledge and understanding.

Lane 1
▾
🔒 Configuring for Lane 1

Highly effective Lane 1 assessment. The real-time, in-person nature makes AI assistance impossible during the examination itself. Works well as a standalone assessment or as a follow-up to a submitted piece of work to verify authorship.

🤝 Configuring for Lane 2

A viva-style conversation could function in Lane 2 if conducted remotely and without invigilation conditions — though its natural strength is as a secure verification tool.

Presentation

Spoken delivery of findings, arguments, or creative work to an audience.

Lane 1 Lane 2
▾
🔒 Configuring for Lane 1

In-person delivery without notes, or with limited permitted materials, creates a Lane 1 condition. Adding live Q&A questions strengthens the verification. Ensure students cannot simply read a pre-prepared AI-generated script.

🤝 Configuring for Lane 2

Pre-recorded presentations are Lane 2 by default. Focus marking on the quality of the argument, use of evidence, and communication design — not simply the content, which AI can generate. Require a process reflection on how the presentation was developed.

Observed Practical / Lab Assessment

Lane 1
▾

🤝 Configuring for Lane 2

Where a written or analytical component accompanies a practical (e.g. a lab report), that element can be designed as Lane 2 — with the practical itself anchoring Lane 1 coverage.

Lane 1 Lane 2

🤝 Configuring for Lane 2

Online or asynchronous simulation tasks — for example, responding to a case scenario in writing or via recorded video — function as Lane 2. Well-designed Lane 2 simulations can ask students to reflect critically on how they used any AI tools in their decision-making.

Portfolio & Process Assessments

Assessments that capture learning over time, including process, iteration, and reflection.

Portfolio

A curated collection of work demonstrating learning, development, or professional practice.

Lane 2
▾
🔒 Configuring for Lane 1

A portfolio is not securable in its standard form. To incorporate Lane 1 coverage, pair with an in-person portfolio defence or viva, where the student must speak to specific pieces without preparation time.

🤝 Configuring for Lane 2

Excellent Lane 2 format. The breadth of evidence, reflective commentary, and documentation of process make quality AI-generated substitution very difficult. Require students to include a rationale for piece selection and a reflective commentary on their development.

Research Project / Dissertation

Sustained independent investigation leading to a substantial written output.

Lane 2
▾
🔒 Configuring for Lane 1

The written dissertation itself is not securable. The standard mitigation is a viva or oral defence of the project. This is widely used at postgraduate level and works well as a Lane 1 accountability moment alongside a Lane 2 written component.

🤝 Configuring for Lane 2

Large-scale original research projects with unique data, fieldwork, or primary inquiry are among the most robust Lane 2 assessments. Focus marking on research design decisions, data interpretation, and critical synthesis — areas where AI cannot replicate genuine inquiry.

Digital & Creative Assessments

Technology-enabled, multimodal, or creative outputs including digital artefacts and media.

AI-Assisted Task

A task explicitly designed around student use of AI tools, with critical evaluation of outputs.

Lane 2
▾
🔒 Configuring for Lane 1

Not typically a Lane 1 assessment type. Could be paired with an in-person debrief or oral element where students must critically discuss their AI use and the decisions they made without AI assistance.

🤝 Configuring for Lane 2

Core Lane 2 assessment type. Ask students to document their AI prompting process, evaluate the quality of AI outputs, and explain what they added, changed, or rejected. Mark on critical judgment and AI literacy, not just the final product.

Online Quiz / Computer-Based Test

Automated, digitally delivered question set — multiple choice, short answer, or adaptive.

Lane 1 Lane 2
▾
🔒 Configuring for Lane 1

Invigilated computer-based tests delivered in a controlled environment are Lane 1. Randomised question banks, time limits, and supervised conditions all strengthen the Lane 1 condition. Avoid purely knowledge-recall formats — test application and analysis where possible.

🤝 Configuring for Lane 2

Open online quizzes are Lane 2 by default. They work well as formative learning tools rather than high-stakes summative assessment. If used summatively in Lane 2, design questions that require application and synthesis rather than simple recall.
