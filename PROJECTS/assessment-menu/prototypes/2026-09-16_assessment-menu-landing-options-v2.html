      Understanding the two-lane approach

      AI use can't be reliably detected, so Cardiff uses a two-lane approach, first developed at the University of Sydney. Assessment has two complementary purposes:

      Lane 1 · Secure assurance of learningTrustworthy evidence of what students have achieved.
      Lane 2 · Open assessment for and as learningSupports learning in open settings, including responsible use of AI.

      What you're assessing decides the lane, not the assessment type, so each entry shows both.

      Cardiff's two-lane approachWhere it comes from (Sydney)

      AI, inclusion and student choice Proposed key message D1

      The two-lane approach doesn't assume students must use generative AI. Learning outcomes should stay achievable without it, and students shouldn't be disadvantaged if they choose not to use it, for ethical, environmental, personal, accessibility or academic reasons. Where AI is allowed, the brief should say whether it's optional or part of the task.

            Assessment Menu

            Designing meaningful, inclusive and future-focused assessment

            At Cardiff University, assessment should let students show they have achieved their programme learning outcomes, through tasks that are authentic, inclusive and aligned with what we want our graduates to develop.

            This menu is a design tool, not a list of recommended assessment replacements. Work through the questions, then compare how each assessment type works in Lane 1 and Lane 2.

            Lane 1 · Secure assurance of learningTrustworthy evidence of what students have achieved.
            Lane 2 · Open assessment for and as learningSupports learning in open settings, including responsible use of AI.
            About the two lanes

            Design with six questions

            Rather than asking "What assessment should I use?", ask: Briefs sit inside questions 4 to 6

            1What do students need to demonstrate?

            Start from the intended learning outcomes: what students should know, understand and be able to do at this point in the programme.

            2Why is this assessment the most appropriate way of evidencing learning?

            No single type is better than another. Compare a few that could show the same learning.

            In each entry: What is it? · What's it for?
            3How authentic is the task to the discipline, profession, or wider context?

            Authenticity depends on the task and the learning it evidences, not on a particular format.

            In each entry: Authenticity
            4How can barriers to participation and success be reduced?
            In each entry: Inclusive design and reasonable adjustments
