Typography leads the page.

Headings with weight.

Readable text with room to breathe.

Franklin Gothic/system for display; system sans for reading and controls. Georgia is a rare numerical accent, not a second body face.

Small, deliberate controls.

Square corners softened by 3px, clear labels and stable borders. Colour changes acknowledge interaction without distracting movement.

Icons explain an action.

Use → for next, ← for previous, ↓ for an in-page destination, ↗ for leaving this page, and ✓ with a text status. Keep icons beside labels; name icon-only buttons for assistive technology.

The reviewed prototypes use these small symbols, not an icon library. For additional pictograms, the vault’s Cardiff brand guidance specifies Ionicons Sharp.

Space and rules create hierarchy.

Use the 4px scale. Group related controls closely; give new ideas a larger gap. Separate entries with a thin rule instead of boxing every item.

Spacing rhythm

8–12px inside controls · 16–24px within a group · 28–40px between columns · 44–80px between sections.

Desktop container: up to 1240px, with 48px margins. Small screens: 16–20px margins and stacked columns.

1px neutral rule · repeated rows

2px charcoal rule · major boundary

No shadows or raised cards in v1. Red rules are occasional focal marks, not the default divider.

Six questions to design with

Keep the learning outcomes at the centre of the conversation.

What do students need to demonstrate?

Start from your learning outcomes. Identify the knowledge, skills and judgement students need to show.

Learning outcomes first

Why is this assessment the most appropriate way of evidencing learning?

Choose a task that makes the intended learning visible. Compare what each assessment type is for before choosing a format.

In each entry: What is it? · What’s it for?

How authentic is the task to the discipline, profession, or wider context?

Consider the decisions, practices and audiences that matter in your discipline. Ask what makes this task meaningful.

In each entry: Authenticity

How can barriers to participation and success be reduced?

Consider access, participation and reasonable adjustments from the start, including the demands of the chosen format.

In each entry: Inclusive design and reasonable adjustments

What role, if any, should GenAI play in the assessment process?

Let the learning outcomes guide the lane. Make expectations about AI clear in the assessment brief.

In each entry: AI considerations · Lane 1 and Lane 2

How does this assessment contribute to the wider programme assessment journey?

Look across modules for progression, manageable deadlines and a balance of assessment types. Discuss the pattern with your programme team.

Think across the programme

Notes stay in this open player, including when you revisit this component. Reloading or closing the player clears them. Copy them to keep a record. Nothing is sent to a server.

Guidance, not policy.

Decisions about assessment types and lanes sit with module and programme teams, based on learning outcomes. These questions support discussion; they do not calculate a recommendation.

AI, inclusion and student choice

Proposed D1 · for discussion

The proposed position is that the two-lane approach does not assume students must use generative AI, learning outcomes should remain achievable without it, and students should not be disadvantaged if they choose not to use it.
