Understanding the two-lane approach

AI use can't be reliably detected, so Cardiff uses a two-lane approach, first developed at the University of Sydney. Assessment has two complementary purposes:

Lane 1 · Secure assurance of learningTrustworthy evidence of what students have achieved.
Lane 2 · Open assessment for and as learningSupports learning in open settings, including responsible use of AI.

What you're assessing decides the lane, not the assessment type, so each entry shows both.

AI, inclusion and student choice

The two-lane approach doesn't assume students must use generative AI. Learning outcomes should stay achievable without it, and students shouldn't be disadvantaged if they choose not to use it, for ethical, environmental, personal, accessibility or academic reasons. Where AI is allowed, the brief should say whether it's optional or part of the task.

Thinking at programme level

Students experience assessment as a programme, not a series of separate modules. Look across the programme for a balance of assessment types, manageable deadlines, progression, and consistent expectations about AI.
