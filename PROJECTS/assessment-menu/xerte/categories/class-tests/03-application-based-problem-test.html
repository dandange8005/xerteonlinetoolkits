What is it?

An in-class test requiring students to apply knowledge to unseen problems, case studies, or data sets.

What's it for?

  • Assessing application and reasoning
  • Evaluating problem-solving skills

AI considerations

This assessment type has strong protection against AI misuse due to unseen, contextual questions delivered in person.

  • Does GenAI risk undermining the intended learning outcome?
  • Can the work be confidently attributed to the student if not invigilated?
  • How susceptible is this task to AI misuse?
  • If AI is permitted, are expectations and transparency requirements clear?
  • Can a reflective or process‑evidence element reduce AI substitution?

Lane 1 and Lane 2

Lane 1

Secure assurance of learning

  • Create an in-class, supervised test where students apply knowledge to unseen problems
  • Controlled conditions to demonstrate independent learning.
Example

Students complete a timed, invigilated case-based test where they diagnose a problem and justify their answer without external support.

Lane 2

Open assessment for and as learning

  • Open, take-home assessment where students apply knowledge to complex or unfamiliar problems.
Example

Students analyse a real-world data set and produce a report interpreting trends and proposing solutions, drawing on course concepts and external tools where appropriate.

Practical considerations

Content needed: rooms and lab space, staff time and marking load at scale, timetabling, and resourcing adjustments.

Authenticity

Students value tests that reflect ‘real’ problem solving rather than memorisation. Application-based problem tests allow learners to demonstrate understanding by applying concepts to practical, often unfamiliar scenarios, mirroring how knowledge is used in real-world contexts. This approach not only promotes deeper learning but also better prepares students for professional and academic challenges beyond the classroom.

Inclusive design and reasonable adjustments

Application-based problem tests should be designed to support students in demonstrating their reasoning as well as their final answers, by structuring tasks in ways that make expectations clear and manageable. Think about:

  • Providing step-by-step scaffolding within questions
  • Avoiding overloading a single test with too many tasks
  • Ensuring that expectations around method and working are made explicit
