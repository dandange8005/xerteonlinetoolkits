What is it?

A diagnostic or formative class test using Mentimeter. This would be a live, in-class diagnostic activity rather than a formal assessment method, using Mentimeter to gather students' responses to conceptual, reflective or application-based questions.

What's it for?

  • Checking conceptual understanding
  • Supporting dialogue and peer learning
  • Identifying threshold concepts and misunderstandings

AI considerations

In-class Mentimeter activities typically limit unsupervised AI use, supporting more authentic insight into student thinking. When used outside class (e.g. asynchronous polls), students may use AI to help formulate responses, which can be leveraged as a learning support tool rather than restricted.

  • Does GenAI risk undermining the intended learning outcome?
  • Can the work be confidently attributed to the student if not invigilated?
  • How susceptible is this task to AI misuse?
  • If AI is permitted, are expectations and transparency requirements clear?
  • Can a reflective or process‑evidence element reduce AI substitution?

Lane 1 and Lane 2

Lane 1

Secure assurance of learning

  • Run Mentimeter questions live in a timetabled session
  • Require students to respond individually to unseen questions without external tools.
Example

In a Cardiff University biosciences lecture, students respond to a series of concept-check questions about a newly introduced process (e.g. cellular respiration stages). Responses are displayed in real time and used to clarify misconceptions immediately.

Lane 2

Open assessment for and as learning

  • Extend Mentimeter activities beyond the live classroom (e.g. pre/post-session or asynchronous tasks)
  • Encourage students to reflect, discuss, and potentially use digital tools (including AI) to refine their thinking.
Example

Students in a Cardiff Business School module respond to an open-ended Mentimeter question before class (e.g. “What factors influence consumer trust in a digital economy?”). In class, responses are analysed collectively, with students comparing perspectives and building on ideas.

Practical considerations New field

Content needed: rooms and lab space, staff time and marking load at scale, timetabling, and resourcing adjustments.

Authenticity

This method mirrors professional practices such as polling and consultation. It encourages students to articulate uncertainty as a normal part of learning.

Inclusive design and reasonable adjustments

When using Mentimeter for in-class tests, inclusive design involves ensuring that all students can engage meaningfully by providing flexible participation options, clear and accessible question design, and appropriate adjustments to accommodate diverse needs and digital access. Think about:

  • Providing alternative ways to participate (e.g. verbal contribution, written submission if digital access is limited).
  • Ensuring questions are clearly worded and accessible.
  • Allowing additional time or repetition for students who need it.
  • Being mindful of digital accessibility (screen readers, device access, connectivity).
