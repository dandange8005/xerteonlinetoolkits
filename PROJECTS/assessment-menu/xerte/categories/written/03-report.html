What is it?

An assessment in which students produce a structured written document addressing a specific task, problem, or scenario, often using disciplinary conventions such as headings, sections, data presentation, and evidence-based recommendations. Reports are typically analytical and applied in focus, and may be based on case studies, research, professional scenarios, or practical investigations.

What's it for?

  • Assessing students’ ability to apply disciplinary knowledge to real or simulated contexts
  • Developing skills in making evidence-based recommendations or decisions
  • Assessing professional and structured written communication
  • Evaluating analytical thinking, problem-solving, and interpretation of data or evidence

AI considerations

Reports are vulnerable to hidden GenAI use where tasks can be completed through generic synthesis or formatting unless design either restricts AI use (Lane 1) or explicitly incorporates and evaluates it (Lane 2). Authenticity is strengthened through context-specific tasks and clear expectations.

  • Does GenAI risk undermining the intended learning outcome?
  • Can the work be confidently attributed to the student if not invigilated?
  • How susceptible is this task to AI misuse?
  • If AI is permitted, are expectations and transparency requirements clear?
  • Can a reflective or process‑evidence element reduce AI substitution?

Lane 1 and Lane 2

Lane 1

Secure assurance of learning

  • Use context-specific, applied, or localised scenarios that are difficult to replicate generically
  • Focus assessment on analysis, interpretation, and justification rather than surface structure
  • Require evidence of process, calculation, or methodology
  • Restrict or clearly define permitted AI use
  • Provide unique datasets, case materials, or prompts
Example

A report based on a provided dataset or case study, where students analyse findings and produce evidence-based recommendations, demonstrating their reasoning and methodology using materials not available outside the assessment.

Lane 2

Open assessment for and as learning

  • Explicitly allow GenAI use in drafting, structuring, or data interpretation
  • Focus marking criteria on judgement, decision-making, and contextual appropriateness
  • Include reflective commentary on strengths and limitations of AI support
  • Assess critical evaluation and refinement of AI-generated outputs
  • Require students to document and explain how AI tools were used
Example

A professional report where students may use GenAI to support structuring or initial drafting, but must critically evaluate outputs, adapt them to the context, and include a commentary explaining how AI contributed to and was refined within their work.

Practical considerations New field

Content needed: rooms and lab space, staff time and marking load at scale, timetabling, and resourcing adjustments.

Authenticity

Reports closely mirror professional practices across many disciplines, requiring students to analyse information, communicate findings clearly, and make justified recommendations. This makes them highly authentic assessments. Authenticity is strongest when tasks reflect realistic contexts and prioritise interpretation, judgement, and decision-making over generic structure or formatting.

Inclusive design and reasonable adjustments

Reports can present accessibility challenges related to structuring extended writing, interpreting task requirements, or managing complex information and data.

Inclusive design strategies:

  • Provide clear guidance on structure, format, and expectations
  • Use transparent marking criteria focused on analysis and reasoning
  • Scaffold tasks with staged submissions or checkpoints where appropriate
  • Ensure manageable scope and clarity in task requirements
  • Where group work is involved (e.g. collaborative research reports), carefully plan how students are allocated to groups and how groups are managed, taking individual needs into consideration

Reasonable adjustments:

  • Allow extended deadlines or additional time where appropriate
  • Enable use of assistive technologies (e.g. text-to-speech, formatting tools)
  • Offer alternative formats where needed
