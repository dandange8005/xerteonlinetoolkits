What is it?

An assessment in which students produce a structured written response to a question or prompt, drawing on relevant sources, theories, and evidence to construct and support an argument. Essays may vary in length and formality and are typically completed independently over a defined period, either under timed conditions or as coursework.

What's it for?

  • Assessing students’ ability to construct and communicate coherent arguments in writing
  • Developing academic writing, reasoning, and evaluative judgement
  • Assessing critical analysis, interpretation, and synthesis of sources
  • Evaluating understanding and application of disciplinary knowledge

AI considerations

Essays are particularly susceptible to hidden GenAI use unless tasks are either tightly controlled (Lane 1) or explicitly designed to incorporate and critically engage with AI (Lane 2). Clear expectations and assessment design are essential to ensure validity.

  • Does GenAI risk undermining the intended learning outcome?
  • Can the work be confidently attributed to the student if not invigilated?
  • How susceptible is this task to AI misuse?
  • If AI is permitted, are expectations and transparency requirements clear?
  • Can a reflective or process‑evidence element reduce AI substitution?

Lane 1 and Lane 2

Lane 1

Secure assurance of learning

  • Use timed, in-person or controlled conditions where appropriate
  • Focus assessment on argument, reasoning, and use of evidence rather than fluency alone
  • Require use of specific or provided sources
  • Restrict or clearly define permitted AI use
  • Set unseen or tightly framed questions
Example

A timed, invigilated essay in which students respond to an unseen question, drawing on their knowledge to construct a structured argument and demonstrate independent critical thinking under controlled conditions.

Lane 2

Open assessment for and as learning

  • Explicitly incorporate GenAI into research, drafting, or idea generation
  • Focus marking criteria on interpretation, judgement, and originality of thinking
  • Include reflective or justificatory commentary on process
  • Assess students’ ability to critique, refine, and build on AI outputs
  • Require transparency about how AI tools were used
Example

An essay where students may use GenAI to support idea generation or drafting, but must submit a reflective commentary explaining how AI was used, critically evaluating outputs and demonstrating how they developed their own argument.

Practical considerations

Content needed: rooms and lab space, staff time and marking load at scale, timetabling, and resourcing adjustments.

Authenticity

Essays support the development of core academic skills, including constructing arguments, engaging critically with sources, and communicating complex ideas effectively in writing. These skills are transferable across many professional contexts. Authenticity is strongest when essays prioritise disciplinary reasoning, evidence use, and critical judgement over surface-level fluency.

Inclusive design and reasonable adjustments

Essays can present accessibility challenges related to writing fluency, language, time management, or hidden expectations about academic conventions.

Inclusive design strategies:

  • Provide clear guidance on structure, expectations, and marking criteria
  • Use transparent and well-scaffolded prompts
  • Ensure manageable scope and workload
  • Offer exemplars or models where appropriate

Reasonable adjustments:

  • Allow extended deadlines or additional time where appropriate
  • Enable the use of assistive technologies (e.g. speech-to-text)
  • Provide alternative formats where needed
