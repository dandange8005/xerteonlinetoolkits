    GenAI and assessment design

    GenAI has significant implications for assessment, particularly in open coursework contexts. Rather than attempting to make assessments "AI‑proof", the focus should be on alignment with learning outcomes, authenticity and clarity.

    Effective assessment design asks:

    What needs to be evidenced?

    What knowledge, skills or judgement are students meant to demonstrate?

    How will learning be shown?

    How will students show this in a way that reflects disciplinary practice?

    What role might GenAI play?

    Where might GenAI meaningfully support learning, and where might it undermine it?

    Understanding the two-lane approach

    Lane 1

    Secure assurance of learning

    • Trustworthy judgements about progress and attainment.
    • Integrity of the award and individual learning protected.
    • Controlled conditions, contextual task design, or verification.

    Lane 2

    Open assessment for and as learning

    • Open, supported assessment outside controlled settings.
    • AI use cannot be prohibited in unsecured contexts.
    • Focus on ethical, critical and discipline-specific use.

    In the absence of Cardiff‑specific assessment classifications, the University is currently drawing on the University of Sydney's two‑lane assessment model as a helpful framing device when communicating expectations to students.

    The Two-Lane approach does not assume that students must use GenAI. Where AI is permitted within assessment, programme teams should make clear whether its use is optional or an integral part of the task. Assessment should be designed so that students are not disadvantaged if they choose not to use AI for ethical, environmental, personal, accessibility, or academic reasons. Please see the assessment menu types for examples of Lane 1 and 2 assessments.
