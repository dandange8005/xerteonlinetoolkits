Heading Hierarchy

Cardiff University uses a clear heading hierarchy to structure content. All headings use the Marr Sans font family.

Standard Headings (H1-H6)

Heading 1 - Main Page Title

Use H1 for the main page title. There should only be one H1 per page for proper document structure and accessibility.

Heading 2 - Major Section Title

H2 headings divide the page into major sections. They are the primary organizational tool for content.

Heading 3 - Subsection Title

H3 headings break down H2 sections into smaller, more specific topics.

Heading 4 - Content Group

H4 headings are used for grouping related content within subsections.

Heading 5 - Minor Heading

H5 headings provide additional structure when needed for complex content.

Heading 6 - Smallest Heading

H6 is the smallest heading level, used sparingly for the finest level of detail.

Special Heading Classes
These classes provide alternative styling for specific use cases.

Section Title with .sectionTitle class

The .sectionTitle class provides distinctive styling for major section breaks.

Content Title with .contentTitle class

The .contentTitle class is used for content area headings.

Page Header with .page-header class

The .page-header class removes top margin, ideal for page titles at the top of content.

The Importance of Typography in Education

Typography plays a crucial role in educational materials. Well-designed text not only enhances readability but also improves comprehension and retention. When students can easily read and understand course materials, they are better equipped to engage with the content and achieve learning outcomes.

Research has shown that proper typography can reduce cognitive load, allowing students to focus on the content rather than struggling to decode poorly formatted text. Factors such as font choice, size, line height, and spacing all contribute to the overall reading experience.

The Cardiff University v2 theme has been specifically designed with these principles in mind. Every typographic decision—from the selection of the Marr Sans font family to the careful calibration of line heights and spacing—serves to create an optimal reading environment for students and educators alike.

Key Typography Principles

Several key principles guide the typography system in this theme. First, consistency ensures that similar elements are styled the same way throughout the interface. Second, hierarchy helps users understand the relationship between different pieces of content. Third, accessibility ensures that all users, regardless of ability, can access and understand the content.

Scientific & Technical Notation

The chemical formula for water is H2O, while carbon dioxide is CO2. Subscripts are commonly used in chemistry and mathematics.

Einstein's famous equation is E=mc2. The area of a square is calculated as side2. Superscripts are used for exponents, footnotes, and ordinal indicators (1st, 2nd, 3rd).

Understanding Climate Change: A Scientific Perspective

Climate change represents one of the most significant challenges facing humanity in the 21st century. This article explores the scientific consensus and evidence supporting our understanding of global climate systems.

Introduction

Climate change, also known as global warming, refers to the long-term shifts in global temperatures and weather patterns. While climate change is a natural phenomenon that has occurred throughout Earth's history, the current period of warming is occurring more rapidly than past events, and scientific evidence overwhelmingly indicates that human activities are the primary cause.

The Greenhouse Effect

The greenhouse effect is a natural process that warms the Earth's surface. When the Sun's energy reaches the Earth's atmosphere, some of it is reflected back to space and the rest is absorbed and re-radiated by greenhouse gases. These gases include:

  • Carbon dioxide (CO2)
  • Methane (CH4)
  • Nitrous oxide (N2O)
  • Water vapor (H2O)

The absorbed energy warms the atmosphere and the surface of the Earth. This process is essential for life as we know it—without it, Earth's average temperature would be about -18°C instead of the current 15°C.

The scientific evidence is clear: climate change is real, it is happening now, and human activities are the primary cause.

Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC)

Human Impact

Since the Industrial Revolution, human activities—particularly the burning of fossil fuels—have increased atmospheric CO2 concentrations by more than 40%. This enhancement of the greenhouse effect is causing global temperatures to rise at an unprecedented rate.

For more information, visit the IPCC website or consult the National Climate Assessment.

Conclusion

Understanding climate change requires engagement with complex scientific concepts and data. However, the fundamental principles are clear, and the evidence is overwhelming. Action is needed now to mitigate the worst effects of climate change and ensure a sustainable future for generations to come.

Reading Considerations

Accessibility Note

All typography in this theme meets WCAG 2.1 Level AA standards for contrast and readability. Font sizes are specified in relative units (rem) to respect user preferences and browser settings.

Optimal Line Length

The ideal line length for readability is generally considered to be 50-75 characters per line. This theme's default content width is optimized for comfortable reading. Lines that are too long can cause readers to lose their place, while lines that are too short create a choppy reading experience.

Line Height & Spacing

Proper line height (leading) is crucial for readability. This theme uses a line height of 1.6 for body text, which provides comfortable spacing between lines without making the text feel disconnected.

Paragraph spacing is also carefully considered. Each paragraph has appropriate margin spacing to clearly delineate separate thoughts while maintaining visual flow.
